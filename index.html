Chapter 1

Introduction

OpenExpense is a privacy-first, open-source expense ledger and mobile-friendly wallet. There are no accounts, no cloud sync, and no data mining — only an encrypted ledger that lives in your browser.

Most expense apps ask you to trust a company with your spending history. OpenExpense takes the opposite approach: the entire app runs on your device, your ledger is encrypted before it is written to storage, and nothing is uploaded to a server because there is no server.

You are always in control. There is no background service, no AI operator, and no automated agent acting on your behalf — every expense is something you log, edit, or confirm yourself.

What you get

  • Calendar ledger — tap any day to log one or more expenses.
  • Monthly summary — paid vs pending totals, trends, top merchants, and a year chart.
  • Receipt scanning — photograph or upload a receipt or PDF; local OCR suggests fields you review before saving.
  • Encrypted autosave — changes write to encrypted local storage automatically.
  • Encrypted export — download a ledger.json plus key.json you can move to another device.
  • Spending report PDF — export the current month from the sidebar.

Open source & transparent

The full codebase is on GitHub under the MIT license. You can read exactly how encryption, storage, and receipt parsing work. The project is free to use — no subscriptions, ads, or upsells.

Device-agnostic wallet

The same encrypted wallet works in any modern mobile or desktop browser. There is no app store install and no hardware wallet to pair. Export the encrypted ledger and keep key.json on your device; open both files on the next phone or laptop. OpenExpense never stores that portable key in browser cache.

Privacy by architecture

Because OpenExpense never calls a backend, your records cannot be harvested in a corporate breach or sold to advertisers. Encryption keys are created on your device and marked non-extractable, so even casual inspection of browser storage shows only ciphertext. On shared or untrusted devices, you can pause autosave from the header disk button so changes stay in memory only for that session.

Chapter 2

Using the app

Everything in OpenExpense is designed around the calendar. This chapter walks through logging expenses, reading your monthly summary, scanning receipts, and managing backups.

Ledger name & status

Name your ledger at the top of the Expenses view (for example, Personal expenses). The name appears in exports and PDF reports. Under the name, two pills show status: You own your data (green when encryption and autosave are on; amber if autosave is paused or encryption is unavailable) and File loaded / Not loaded (whether a named ledger or any expenses are present).

The calendar

The main view is a month calendar. Use the arrows to change months, or tap Today to jump back to the current month. Days with expenses show a total; tap a day to open its detail panel.

Toolbar actions on the calendar:

  • Import — open files from your OpenExpense folder. On iPhone and Android you can select the ledger and key.json together. Older zip backups still work.
  • Export — writes both files into an OpenExpense folder (default). Long-press Export to pick a different folder. On iPhone and Android, Save to Files and choose that folder.
  • Clear — wipe all expenses and the ledger name on this device (with confirmation).
  • Scan — photograph or upload a receipt or PDF for local OCR.

Logging an expense

Tap a calendar day to open the expense panel. For each entry you can set:

  • Entry type — expense or income. The right-hand card flips to match.
  • Title — merchant, paycheck, or description.
  • Cost / amount — dollars.
  • Recurring — when checked, a How often? prompt asks for monthly, every 2 months, or quarterly. Copies land on the same day of those later months for about a year.
  • Paid — mark whether the charge is settled or still pending.
  • Notes — optional context (line items, invoice numbers, etc.).

Same-day charges with the same title are grouped together. Recurring payments show a series badge. When you delete a re-accruing payment, check Remove all recurring copies of this payment to wipe the whole series — leave it unchecked to remove only that day.

Monthly summary sidebar

On wider screens, the right sidebar shows a spending report for the month you are viewing:

  • Total spent with paid vs pending breakdown.
  • Insights — averages, month-over-month trend, recurring vs one-time spend, and a projected total for the current month.
  • Top spend — your largest merchants by amount.
  • Year overview — bar chart of monthly totals; click a bar to jump to that month.
  • This month — pending and paid entries; click any row to open that day.

Use the PDF button in the sidebar header to download a formatted monthly spending report.

Receipt scanning

Tap Scan on the calendar toolbar or the floating scan button. Paper receipts, PDF invoices, emailed bills, and screenshots all work. On first use, the app downloads a local OCR engine (~5 MB) and caches it in your browser — processing never leaves your device. The reader enhances low-light photos and prefers labeled totals so tax lines are less likely to be mistaken for the amount due.

After scanning, a review sheet shows suggested merchant, amount, date, and notes. Edit anything before saving; nothing is written to your ledger until you tap Save expense. You can also choose Save & scan another to log multiple receipts in a row. Expand View raw scanned text if you want to verify what the parser read.

Receipt parsing is a convenience, not an autopilot. You are always the one who decides what gets saved — the app never auto-logs a scanned receipt without your confirmation.

Header controls

  • Disk button — toggle autosave. When off, changes stay in memory only until you turn autosave back on or export a backup.
  • Sun/moon button — switch between light and dark theme.
  • Open Source — link to the GitHub repository.

Chapter 3

Your data

Because OpenExpense runs entirely in your browser, you are the only person who can access your financial records. This chapter covers how data is stored, backed up, and structured.

How your data is stored

Your ledger is saved in IndexedDB and encrypted with AES-256-GCM before it touches disk. The key is created on this device, marked non-extractable, and stored locally — inspecting browser storage shows only ciphertext.

Autosave writes every change to encrypted local storage automatically (debounced, so rapid edits batch into one write). The disk button in the header pauses autosave for a session where changes stay in memory only.

Encryption requires a secure context (https:// or localhost). Open the app through a local server or HTTPS — not by double-clicking the HTML file — so encryption works. Only non-sensitive UI preferences (theme, autosave on/off, first-visit flag) use localStorage; the ledger itself never does.

Backups & moving between devices

Export is your manual backup. It writes two files next to each other in an OpenExpense folder: an encrypted .json and a key.json. You can pick a different folder after the first save (long-press Export). The portable key is never stored in the browser cache. Autosave still encrypts a copy in this browser with a separate, non-extractable device key.

  1. Default folder — OpenExpense inside Documents (or the folder you pick). iPhone and Android: use the share sheet → Save to Files → OpenExpense.
  2. Keep the pair — the key unlocks only that ledger file (they share an id). Neither file is useful alone.
  3. Import — select one file or both from that folder. Unencrypted JSON is imported only after you confirm, then it lives encrypted here.

There is no cloud sync. Move backups manually — USB drive, encrypted cloud folder, or any method you trust.

Ledger format

Inside the encrypted record, calendar dates map to arrays of expense and income objects (income uses "kind": "income"). After decryption this is the same shape as a legacy plaintext import: 

{
  "name": "GBA Expenses",
  "events": {
    "2026-06-03": [
      { "title": "API Hosting", "price": 49.99, "recurring": true, "paid": false, "note": "" }
    ]
  }
}

Each expense has a title, price, and optional recurring, paid, and note fields. Dates use YYYY-MM-DD keys.

Security notes

  • Device-bound keys protect against casual inspection, not a compromised browser with full access.
  • Backup often — export the encrypted ledger.json and keep the matching key.json.
  • Split sensitive sets — keep key and ciphertext apart when storing or sending.
  • No syncing — move exports manually between computers.

Chapter 4

Updates

What changed in OpenExpense recently. The product is still two screens, still offline, and still encrypted on this device. A dated list also lives in CHANGELOG.md on GitHub.

17 August 2026 — OpenExpense folder

Export now defaults to an OpenExpense folder. On a computer that folder is created (or reused) under Documents. On iPhone and Android, share both files at once and save them into OpenExpense in the Files app. Long-press Export to pick another folder. Import can open the ledger and key together.

17 August 2026 — Square controls

The header mark and every icon button — month arrows, toolbar actions, GitHub, autosave, theme, close, and scan — now share one 40×40 square so the chrome lines up.

17 August 2026 — SEO, branding, and trust

Search and social cards now describe OpenExpense as an open-source expense ledger and mobile-friendly wallet. The header lockup, welcome trust row, and status line make the local-only security model easier to see. Layout containers are unchanged.

17 August 2026 — Encrypted files

Export now saves an encrypted ledger.json (expenses and income in the same file) and a key.json next to it. When you open a ledger, the app asks for that key. The key is not saved in this browser — only in the file you download.

17 August 2026 — Income

Use the Expenses / Income switch on the right-hand card. The card flips like a coin. The calendar stays the same grid: expense pills stay indigo, income pills are green. Days with both use a split highlight. Log either type from a calendar day.

17 August 2026 — Recurring cadence

Recurring is no longer monthly-only. After you check Recurring, choose Monthly, Bi-monthly (every 2 months), or Quarterly. Older entries without a cadence still behave as monthly.

17 August 2026

Design refresh, smarter recurring payments, better receipt reading, and clearer status pills. Tracked in pull request #83.

  • Look and feel — Shared light and dark tokens, quieter glass surfaces, and larger tap targets on phones.
  • Recurring payments — Same-title charges group on a day. When you delete a re-accruing payment, you can remove every copy at once.
  • Receipts — Paper photos, screenshots, and PDF invoices. The reader prefers a labeled total and waits for you to confirm before saving.
  • Header mark — Graphic card icon only (no wordmark). The header still announces OpenExpense to assistive tech.
  • You own your data — Green when this browser can encrypt and autosave is on.
  • File loaded / Not loaded — Green when a named ledger or any expenses are present; red when the calendar is still empty.
