Chapter 1

Introduction

OpenExpense is a privacy-first, open-source expense ledger and mobile-friendly wallet. There are no accounts, no cloud sync, and no data mining — only an encrypted ledger that lives in your browser.

Most expense apps ask you to trust a company with your spending history. OpenExpense takes the opposite approach: the entire app runs on your device, your ledger is encrypted before it is written to storage, and nothing is uploaded to a server because there is no server.

You are always in control. There is no background service, no AI operator, and no automated agent acting on your behalf — every expense is something you log, edit, or confirm yourself.

What you get

  • Overview — potential savings, daily safe spend, deposited vs expected, and paid vs pending for the month.
  • Tracker — calendar, month register, and add / scan / search. Filter All, Expenses, or Income.
  • Planner — tax withhold, savings holds, 50/30/20, and daily safe spend.
  • Privacy — local backup, import, clear, and this help.
  • Calendar ledger — tap any day to log one or more expenses or income.
  • Search — / or Ctrl/Cmd+K. Filter with group:, tag: or cat:, is:unpaid, amounts, and dates.
  • Monthly summary — paid vs pending totals, trends, top merchants, and a year chart.
  • Receipt scanning — photograph or upload a receipt or PDF; local OCR suggests fields you review before saving.
  • Encrypted autosave — changes write to encrypted local storage automatically.
  • Encrypted export — download a ledger.json plus key.json you can move to another device.
  • Monthly statement PDF — export an invoice-style spending or income statement for the month on screen. Built on this device.

Open source & transparent

The full codebase is on GitHub under the MIT license. You can read exactly how encryption, storage, and receipt parsing work. The project is free to use — no subscriptions, ads, or upsells.

Device-agnostic wallet

The same encrypted wallet works in any modern mobile or desktop browser. There is no app store install and no hardware wallet to pair. Export the encrypted ledger and keep key.json on your device; open both files on the next phone or laptop. OpenExpense never stores that portable key in browser cache.

Privacy by architecture

Because OpenExpense never calls a backend, your records cannot be harvested in a corporate breach or sold to advertisers. Encryption keys are created on your device and marked non-extractable, so even casual inspection of browser storage shows only ciphertext. On shared or untrusted devices, you can pause autosave from the header disk button so changes stay in memory only for that session.

Chapter 2

Using the app

Everything in OpenExpense is designed around the calendar. This chapter walks through logging expenses, reading your monthly summary, scanning receipts, and managing backups.

Search, category tags, and groups

Open search from Tracker, or press / or Ctrl/Cmd+K. Keys stay attached to the words after them, including a space after the colon: group: Rome trip, tag: groceries, cat:food (every tag in that family), is:unpaid, >50, 2026-08. tag: and category: mean the same as cat:. Typing a key alone offers matching names.

Category is a tag you type: Enter creates a new one if it does not exist. On a day, select several rows or drop one onto another to group them. Ungroup clears only the group — price, date, and the label stay. Click a sidebar group or category to open that search.

17 August 2026 — Dashboard groups and plain-language labels

The top blocks are grouped into Account overview and Upcoming & savings, each with a short explanation. The sidebar now uses “This month at a glance,” “Where your money went,” and “Year at a glance.”

Four-tab shell

The bottom bar is Overview, Tracker, Planner, and Privacy. The calendar and month register stay on the main view under every app tab. Overview adds the cash snapshot above that board: potential savings, daily safe spend, deposited vs expected, paid vs pending, and the year income and spending lines. Tracker is the same board with All / Expenses / Income in focus. Planner holds tax withhold, savings holds, 50/30/20, and daily safe spend above the same calendar. Privacy is this help plus backup, import, and clear.

Account math is unchanged: Deposited is income you have ticked as deposited this month, so a paycheck still sitting on the calendar does not count as cash. Potential Savings is those deposits less everything the month spends, paid or still owed — unless Planner settings change which income and bills count, withhold tax, or hold savings out of that leftover. Savings funds is the reserve behind that: what every earlier month settled at, up to today. When a month spends past its deposits, Potential Savings goes negative and the difference comes out of Savings funds. Month net is income minus spending, counting income whether or not it has landed. Unpaid paychecks show as expected on Overview and on the Tracker income register.

Planner settings

The Planner tab is the third item on the bottom bar. Potential Savings is one waterfall on whole cents: counted income, minus a tax withhold, minus a savings hold, minus counted spend. Defaults keep the original cash line (deposits minus every logged bill).

Tax withhold is a percent of counted income. 15.3 is the IRS self-employment tax rate (12.4 Social Security + 2.9 Medicare; Topic 554 / Pub 334). 25 and 30 are common quarterly-estimate placeholders used with Pub 505 — they are not a tax return. Weekly savings becomes this month’s share (weekly × days in the month ÷ 7). Weekly income goal is the pace a Sunday–Saturday row has to beat for a green hint; leave it blank to use this month’s own gross income split by days. A monthly dollar and a percent of after-tax income stack with that weekly hold once. Current savings is optional: the amount already in the bank. It does not change leftover. When you enter it, Overview replaces the leftover ring with a growth-potential percent (leftover ÷ current savings). The CFPB 50/30/20 split (Warren and Tyagi, All Your Worth, 2005) is a scoreboard against after-tax income, not a second deduction. Needs are Housing, Utilities, Health, Transit, and Groceries. Wants are Dining, Coffee, Entertainment, Shopping, Travel, and Subscriptions.

Daily safe spend is leftover divided by the days still on this month, including today. Days of cash is savings funds plus leftover, divided by this month’s daily burn (counted spend ÷ days elapsed) — the same cash ÷ burn-rate runway used in corporate finance. Week bars are calendar days 1–7, 8–14, 15–21, 22–28, and 29–end. Category monthly caps still use the existing editor. The plan is saved with your ledger and travels with an export.

Quick actions

On Tracker, plus, camera, search, and download sit beside the All / Expenses / Income filter. Hover or long-press for the name. After a folder is linked, download becomes a disk and saves over the existing JSON pair. Backup, import, and clear also live on Privacy.

Status badges

The header holds status next to the brand: You own your data (encryption and autosave on; amber if autosave is paused or encryption is unavailable) and File loaded / Not loaded (whether a named ledger or any expenses are present). After a delete or clear, Undo sits next to File loaded for about ten seconds. GitHub, the autosave disk, and the theme toggle stay on the right.

The calendar

The main view is a month calendar. Use the arrows to change months, or tap the calendar icon to jump back to the current month. Days with items show that day’s net (up or down); tap a day to open its sheet. Drag a chip onto another day to move those entries. On a phone that sheet snaps to the bottom and shows spent vs income before the list. A day amount turns red when that day’s spending is over the daily safe amount. A thin rail sits on the left of each Sunday–Saturday row only when two or more days in that week are over: half red and half gray for two days, full red for three or more. In-budget weeks stay unmarked. The day squares stay the surface colour. The check reruns when the month loads and when an expense or planner rule changes.

Toolbar icons on the calendar (names are on hover):

  • Import — open files from your OpenExpense folder. On iPhone and Android you can select the ledger and key.json together. Older zip backups still work.
  • Export / Save — first time, writes both files into an OpenExpense folder. After that folder is linked, the download icon becomes a disk and overwrites the existing JSON pair. Long-press to pick a different folder. On iPhone and Android, Save to Files and choose that folder.
  • Clear — wipe all expenses and the ledger name on this device (with confirmation). Undo stays next to File loaded for a few seconds.
  • Scan — photograph or upload a receipt or PDF for local OCR.

Logging an expense

Tap a calendar day to open the expense panel. For each entry you can set:

  • Entry type — expense or income. The right-hand card flips to match.
  • Title — merchant, paycheck, or description.
  • Cost / amount — dollars. On a recurring entry, a new amount updates every copy.
  • Date — when you edit an entry, you can move it. A recurring date change shifts every copy by the same number of days.
  • Recurring — when checked, a How often? prompt asks for weekly, monthly, every 2 months, or quarterly. Weekly copies land on the same weekday; the others keep the same calendar day. About a year of copies is added.
  • Paid — mark whether the charge is settled or still pending.
  • Notes — optional context (line items, invoice numbers, etc.).

Entries stay in the order you put them. Drag the handle to reorder, or focus it and use the arrow keys. On the month grid, drag a chip onto another day to move that copy — other days in a recurring series stay put. Recent titles show as chips under the add form and fill the last amount. Each row can mark paid, duplicate as a one-off on this day, edit, or delete. Previous / next arrows jump days without closing the sheet.

Recurring payments show a series badge. Editing one copy updates the title, date, amount, and cadence on every copy; paid stays on each day. When you delete a re-accruing payment, choose only this day, every Saturday (or whichever weekday you opened), or every copy, all days. “Every Saturday” does not remove Monday or Friday copies. After any removal, Undo appears next to File loaded and on the day sheet for about ten seconds.

Monthly summary sidebar

On wider screens, the right sidebar shows a spending report for the month you are viewing:

  • Total spent with paid vs pending breakdown.
  • This month at a glance — daily average, average entry, change from last month, recurring total, and an estimated month total.
  • Where your money went — your largest merchants by amount.
  • Year at a glance — labeled month columns; tap a column to jump to that month. Paid vs pending is a two-block track.
  • This month — pending and paid entries; click any row to open that day.

Use the PDF button in the sidebar header to download a statement for the face you are viewing. The file is an invoice-style register: issued date, period, ledger name, paid vs unpaid (or deposited vs expected), every line item, and a totals box that matches the sidebar. It is built on this device; nothing is uploaded.

Receipt scanning

Tap Scan on the calendar toolbar or the floating scan button. Paper receipts, PDF invoices, emailed bills, and screenshots all work. On first use, the app downloads a local OCR engine (~5 MB) and caches it in your browser — processing never leaves your device. The reader enhances low-light photos and prefers labeled totals so tax lines are less likely to be mistaken for the amount due.

After scanning, a review sheet shows suggested merchant, amount, date, and notes. Edit anything before saving; nothing is written to your ledger until you tap Save expense. You can also choose Save & scan another to log multiple receipts in a row. Expand View raw scanned text if you want to verify what the parser read.

Receipt parsing is a convenience, not an autopilot. You are always the one who decides what gets saved — the app never auto-logs a scanned receipt without your confirmation.

Header controls

  • Disk button — toggle autosave. When off, changes stay in memory only until you turn autosave back on or export a backup.
  • Sun/moon button — switch between light and dark theme.
  • GitHub — icon in the header that opens the source repository.

Chapter 3

Your data

Because OpenExpense runs entirely in your browser, you are the only person who can access your financial records. This chapter covers how data is stored, backed up, and structured.

How your data is stored

Your ledger is saved in IndexedDB and encrypted with AES-256-GCM before it touches disk. The key is created on this device, marked non-extractable, and stored locally — inspecting browser storage shows only ciphertext.

Autosave writes every change to encrypted local storage automatically (debounced, so rapid edits batch into one write). The disk button in the header pauses autosave for a session where changes stay in memory only.

Encryption requires a secure context (https:// or localhost). Open the app through a local server or HTTPS — not by double-clicking the HTML file — so encryption works. Only non-sensitive UI preferences (theme, autosave on/off, first-visit flag) use localStorage; the ledger itself never does.

Backups & moving between devices

Export is your manual backup. It writes two files next to each other in an OpenExpense folder: an encrypted .json and a key.json. After that folder is linked, the button becomes Save and updates those same files. Long-press Export to pick a different folder. The portable key is never stored in the browser cache. Autosave still encrypts a copy in this browser with a separate, non-extractable device key.

  1. Default folder — OpenExpense inside Documents (or the folder you pick). iPhone and Android: use the share sheet → Save to Files → OpenExpense.
  2. Keep the pair — the key unlocks only that ledger file (they share an id). Neither file is useful alone.
  3. Import — select one file or both from that folder. Unencrypted JSON is imported only after you confirm, then it lives encrypted here.

There is no cloud sync. Move backups manually — USB drive, encrypted cloud folder, or any method you trust.

Ledger format

Inside the encrypted record, calendar dates map to arrays of expense and income objects (income uses "kind": "income"). After decryption this is the same shape as a legacy plaintext import: 

{
  "name": "GBA Expenses",
  "events": {
    "2026-06-03": [
      { "title": "API Hosting", "price": 49.99, "recurring": true, "paid": false, "note": "" }
    ]
  }
}

Each expense has a title, price, and optional recurring, paid, and note fields. Dates use YYYY-MM-DD keys.

Security notes

  • Device-bound keys protect against casual inspection, not a compromised browser with full access.
  • Backup often — export the encrypted ledger.json and keep the matching key.json.
  • Split sensitive sets — keep key and ciphertext apart when storing or sending.
  • No syncing — move exports manually between computers.
